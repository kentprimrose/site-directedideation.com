Resources / Phase-by-phase references

Sources behind the five-step process.

The main field guide keeps the five-step workflow focused on practical questions. Below is the phase-by-phase reference map of the thinkers, methods, and primary publications behind the Directed Ideation MCP tools.

01 / Frame

Define the real question.

Tools for classifying complexity, separating what the problem is from what it is not, and probing purpose, assumptions, and evidence.

Complexity routing

Cynefin framework

Sorts a situation into Clear/Simple, Complicated, Complex, or Chaotic so later phases match the domain.

  • David J. Snowden & Mary E. Boone, “A Leader’s Framework for Decision Making,” Harvard Business Review (November 2007).
Scope & priority

Kepner-Tregoe Situation Appraisal

Separates what the problem is from what it is not and sets priorities before deeper analysis.

  • Charles H. Kepner & Benjamin B. Tregoe, The Rational Manager (McGraw-Hill, 1965).
Critical inquiry

Socratic questioning & elements of reasoning

Probes purpose, exact questions, observed information, inferences, assumptions, and overlooked viewpoints.

  • Richard Paul & Linda Elder, The Miniature Guide to Critical Thinking: Concepts and Tools (Foundation for Critical Thinking).
02 / Diagnose

Find root causes and structure.

Tools for tracing symptoms to core causes, analyzing variation, and identifying high-leverage system dynamics.

Root cause

Five Whys

Iterative root-cause questioning from concrete observed events while marking observed versus assumed links.

  • Taiichi Ohno, Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production (Productivity Press, 1988; Japanese edition 1978).
Measure & Analyze

Six Sigma DMAIC tools

Pareto charts, cause-and-effect (fishbone) diagrams, control charts, VOC-to-CTQ trees, and SIPOC boundaries.

  • Kaoru Ishikawa, Guide to Quality Control (Asian Productivity Organization, 1968 Japanese / 1976 English edition).
  • Peter S. Pande, Robert P. Neuman, & Roland R. Cavanagh, The Six Sigma Way (McGraw-Hill, 2000).
Causal chains

Current Reality Tree (Logical Thinking Process)

Maps observed Undesirable Effects through cause-and-effect chains to identify the core problem, alongside Goal Trees, Evaporating Clouds, Future Reality Trees, Prerequisite Trees, and Categories of Legitimate Reservation.

  • H. William Dettmer, The Logical Thinking Process: A Systems Approach to Complex Problem Solving (ASQ Quality Press, 2007).
  • Eliyahu M. Goldratt, It’s Not Luck (North River Press, 1994).
System dynamics

Systems thinking & leverage points

Feedback loops, stocks and flows, delays, and leverage points where focused changes shift system outcomes.

  • Donella H. Meadows, Thinking in Systems: A Primer (Chelsea Green Publishing, 2008).
  • Peter M. Senge, The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization (Doubleday, 1990).
03 / Generate

Produce genuinely different options.

Creative divergence, analogy, combinatorial design, contradiction resolution, and unblocking methods.

Divergence

Brainstorming rules & SCAMPER

Deferred-judgment brainstorming checklists and systematic transformations: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, and Reverse.

  • Alex F. Osborn, Applied Imagination (Charles Scribner’s Sons, 1953).
  • Bob Eberle, SCAMPER: Games for Imagination Development (D.O.K. Publishers, 1971; Prufrock Press, 1996).
Analogy & combinations

Synectics analogies & Morphological analysis

Direct, personal, symbolic, and fantasy analogies alongside systematic combination of independent solution dimensions.

  • William J. J. Gordon, Synectics: The Development of Creative Capacity (Harper & Row, 1961).
  • Fritz Zwicky, Discovery, Invention, Research through the Morphological Approach (Macmillan, 1969).
Contradictions

TRIZ inventive principles

Resolves technical and operational contradictions—such as needing X and Y when they conflict—without weak compromise.

  • Genrich Altshuller, 40 Principles: TRIZ Keys to Technical Innovation (Technical Innovation Center, 1997).
Unblocking

Lateral provocation, mental locks & creative roles

Deliberate “po” provocations and movement, unlocking habitual mental locks (seeking the “second right answer”), and separating the Explorer, Artist, Judge, and Warrior roles.

  • Edward de Bono, Lateral Thinking: Creativity Step by Step (Harper & Row, 1970).
  • Roger von Oech, A Whack on the Side of the Head (Warner Books, 1983) and A Kick in the Seat of the Pants (Harper & Row, 1986).
04 / Evaluate

Test options against evidence and scenarios.

Argument auditing, anti-bias hypothesis testing, scenario stress-testing, and parallel perspective reviews.

Argument structure

Toulmin model & Pyramid Principle

Auditing whether claim, grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier, and rebuttal support a recommendation, paired with conclusion-first MECE argument structure.

  • Stephen E. Toulmin, The Uses of Argument (Cambridge University Press, 1958).
  • Barbara Minto, The Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing and Thinking (Minto International, 1987; expanded edition 1996).
Bias reduction

Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)

Weighs each piece of evidence against every competing explanation or option—emphasizing disconfirming evidence to counter confirmation bias.

  • Richards J. Heuer Jr., Psychology of Intelligence Analysis (Center for the Study of Intelligence, Central Intelligence Agency, 1999).
Plausible futures

Scenario planning

Stress-tests candidate options across several distinct plausible futures rather than optimizing for a single forecast.

  • Peter Schwartz, The Art of the Long View (Doubleday, 1991).
  • Kees van der Heijden, Scenarios: The Art of Strategic Conversation (Wiley, 1996).
Parallel thinking

Six Thinking Hats & Direct Attention

Separates White (facts), Red (intuition), Black (caution), Yellow (benefits), Green (alternatives), and Blue (process) perspectives alongside CAF, PMI, and APC.

  • Edward de Bono, Six Thinking Hats (Little, Brown and Company, 1985).
  • Edward de Bono, CoRT Thinking Program / Direct Attention Thinking Tools (CAF, PMI, APC).
05 / Decide & stress-test

Commit and check failure modes.

Weighted decision criteria, probabilistic tradeoffs, prospective failure analysis, risk scoring, and fast adaptive loops.

Decision rigor

Kepner-Tregoe Decision Analysis & Potential Problem Analysis

Applies non-negotiable MUST limits and weighted WANT criteria to alternatives, then maps preventive and contingent actions for likely threats.

  • Charles H. Kepner & Benjamin B. Tregoe, The New Rational Manager (Princeton Research Press, 1981).
Uncertainty

Decision trees & expected value

Quantifies uncertain branches, probabilities, and payoffs—and helps assess when a small test is worth running before committing.

  • Howard Raiffa, Decision Analysis: Introductory Lectures on Choices Under Uncertainty (Addison-Wesley, 1968).
Failure modes

Premortem & FMEA

Works backward from an assumed failure to expose blind spots before launch, and scores failure modes by Severity, Occurrence, and Detection.

  • Gary Klein, “Performing a Project Premortem,” Harvard Business Review (September 2007).
  • AIAG & VDA, AIAG & VDA FMEA Handbook (1st ed., 2019).
Fast adaptation

OODA loop

Observe, Orient, Decide, and Act in rapid cycles under changing conditions, feeding observed evidence back into framing and diagnosis.

  • John R. Boyd, “Patterns of Conflict” (briefing, 1986).
  • Robert Coram, Boyd: The Fighter Pilot Who Changed the Art of War (Little, Brown and Company, 2002).
