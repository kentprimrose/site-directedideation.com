Resources / Sources & references

Sources behind the companion tools.

The Directed Ideation field guide stays focused on finding opportunities, selecting goals, and testing ideas. The MCP’s optional companion tools and broader technique menus draw on the established creative, strategic, and quality-improvement works listed below.

01 / Creative & parallel thinking

Breaking habitual patterns and separating modes of thought.

These works inform the MCP’s lateral-thinking exercises, mental-lock prompts, and parallel evaluation modes.

Edward de Bono

Lateral Thinking, Six Thinking Hats & Direct Attention

Provocations (“po”) and movement, random entry, parallel thinking across the Six Thinking Hats (White, Red, Black, Yellow, Green, and Blue), and CoRT / Direct Attention tools: Consider All Factors (CAF), Plus, Minus, Interesting (PMI), and Alternatives, Possibilities, Choices (APC).

  • Edward de Bono, Lateral Thinking: Creativity Step by Step (Harper & Row, 1970).
  • Edward de Bono, Six Thinking Hats (Little, Brown and Company, 1985).
  • Edward de Bono, CoRT Thinking Program and Direct Attention Thinking Tools (CAF, PMI, APC).
Roger von Oech

Mental Locks & Four Creative Roles

Recognizing and loosening the Ten Mental Locks—such as stopping at the first “right answer,” mistaking conventions for fixed rules, or judging ideas too early—and pacing work deliberately across the Explorer, Artist, Judge, and Warrior roles.

  • Roger von Oech, A Whack on the Side of the Head: How You Can Be More Creative (Warner Books, 1983).
  • Roger von Oech, A Kick in the Seat of the Pants: Using Your Explorer, Artist, Judge, and Warrior to Be More Creative (Harper & Row, 1986).
02 / Strategic & systems thinking

Tracing root causes, dissolving conflicts, and mapping change.

These works inform the MCP’s strategic-thinking tool for systems-level cause-and-effect analysis, conflict resolution, and execution planning.

H. William Dettmer

The Logical Thinking Process

A systems approach structured around what to change, what to change to, and how to cause the change: Goal Tree, Current Reality Tree (CRT), Evaporating Cloud (Conflict Resolution Diagram), Future Reality Tree (FRT) with Negative Branch Reservation, Prerequisite Tree (PRT), Transition Tree (TT), and Categories of Legitimate Reservation (CLR).

  • H. William Dettmer, The Logical Thinking Process: A Systems Approach to Complex Problem Solving (ASQ Quality Press, 2007).
Eliyahu M. Goldratt

Theory of Constraints Thinking Processes

Foundational Theory of Constraints thinking-process lineage from which Dettmer’s streamlined Logical Thinking Process evolved, focusing system improvement on core constraints and underlying conflict assumptions.

  • Eliyahu M. Goldratt, It’s Not Luck (North River Press, 1994).
03 / Quality & framing methods

Separating symptoms from causes and sharpening options.

These works inform the MCP’s requirements-clarification tool and broader stage-filtered technique menus.

Six Sigma & Lean

Root-Cause & Requirements Clarification

Five Whys root-cause inquiry, Voice of the Customer to Critical-to-Quality (VOC-to-CTQ) trees, SIPOC process boundaries, Ishikawa (Fishbone) cause-and-effect diagrams, operational definitions, and Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA).

  • Taiichi Ohno, Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production (Productivity Press, 1988; Japanese edition 1978).
  • Kaoru Ishikawa, Guide to Quality Control (Asian Productivity Organization, 1976).
  • Peter S. Pande, Robert P. Neuman, and Roland R. Cavanagh, The Six Sigma Way (McGraw-Hill, 2000).
Idea Transformation

SCAMPER

A structured checklist—Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, and Reverse—for generating genuinely distinct mechanisms during divergence.

  • Alex F. Osborn, Applied Imagination (Charles Scribner’s Sons, 1953).
  • Bob Eberle, SCAMPER: Games for Imagination Development (D.O.K. Publishers, 1971).
Opportunity Framing

Jobs to Be Done

Understanding opportunities through the progress a person is trying to make in a specific circumstance rather than starting from product features or internal categories.

  • Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, and David S. Duncan, Competing Against Luck (HarperBusiness, 2016).
Risk & Prioritization

Premortem & ICE Scoring

Prospective hindsight (imagining a goal or experiment failed to surface blind spots before acting) and lightweight Impact, Confidence, and Ease scoring when comparing candidate directions.

  • Gary Klein, “Performing a Project Premortem,” Harvard Business Review (September 2007).
  • Sean Ellis, ICE prioritization framework (Impact, Confidence, Ease).
